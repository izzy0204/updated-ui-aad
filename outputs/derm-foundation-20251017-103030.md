## 5) Key Metrics
### Usefulness / Efficacy
- **Goal of Metric(s):** Derm Foundation was evaluated for data-efficient accuracy across a range of skin-related classifications tasks [SOURCE 1]. The goal is to reduce the training data, compute, and technical expertise necessary to develop task-specific models for skin image analysis [SOURCE 1].
- **Result:** Training a linear classifier on Derm-Foundations embeddings were substantially more performant (10-15% increase in accuracy) than doing the same for a standard BiT-M model across different proportions of training data [SOURCE 1]. Models built on top of the Derm Foundation embeddings for dermatology-related tasks achieved significantly higher quality than those built solely on embeddings or fine tuned from BiT-M, particularly when working with smaller training datasets [SOURCE 4].
- **Interpretation:** The results demonstrate that the Derm Foundation tool can serve as a useful starting point to accelerate skin-related modeling tasks [SOURCE 4].
- **Test Type:** Derm Foundation was evaluated for data-efficient accuracy across a range of skin-related classifications tasks [SOURCE 1]. The modeling variants considered were a linear classifier on frozen embeddings from BiT-M, a fine-tuned version of BiT-M, a linear classifier on frozen embeddings from the Derm Foundation API, and a fine-tuned version of the model underlying the Derm Foundation API [SOURCE 4].
- **Testing Data Description:** The SFT (supervised fine-tuned) model was trained data from a limited set of countries (United States, Colombia, Australia, public images) and settings (mostly clinical). [SOURCE 1]
