## 2) Uses & Directions
- **Intended Use & Workflow:**  
  Analyze photos of skin lesions taken with a smartphone and classify them with a risk indication (low/medium/high) and track over a duration of time. Meant to empower the individual to self monitor skin lesions and decide when a medical followup should be considered- should not be used as a diagnostic tool (“you have melanoma”).[8]
- **Purpose:**  
  - Provides access to skin knowledge at an accessible, scalable, and affordable price, especially when access to a dermatologist/specialized health care professional is limited. 
  - Diagnostic accuracy of general practitioners and family physicians in differentiating benign and malignant skin tumors is relatively low 
  - Verified, factual information pertaining to skin health can be provided on demand. 
  - Addresses detection of potential skin cancer due to low rates of professional screening/accessibility, and patient difficulty in recognizing lesions that could be deemed suspicious. 
  - Empowers patients to practice preventative self monitoring. [8]

- **Use Cases:**  
  The SkinVision Service is a software-only, over-the-counter (OTC), mobile
  medical application, which is intended for use on consumer mobile devices by laypeople.

  The SkinVision Service allows users to take and submit photos of skin lesions for assessment, and gives a skin cancer risk indication associated with the specific lesion, detailing whether it is recommended to visit a specialized healthcare professional for further examination of the lesion, or to keep monitoring the lesion within the Standard Of Care.

  The application also facilitates keeping track of skin lesions, and provides information on the photographed lesions that may be used when seeking professional healthcare advice.

  The SkinVision Service augments already existing self-assessment techniques of skin lesions, and is not an alternative to healthcare professionals, who may however use the SkinVision Service.

  The SkinVision Service is not intended for use on persons under the age of 18 years old. The SkinVision Service does not diagnose skin cancer, does not provide any other diagnosis. [6]

- **Primary Intended Users:**  
  “SkinVision is a service of choice whether you want to address your most immediate concerns, learn what steps you should take next, understand your skin risk profile and introduce the most intelligent skin health regimen to your seasonal rhythm.” [13]
- **Example Use Scenarios:**  
  The model fits into direct-to-patient preventative care. Users can take photos of their skin at home, get instant feedback, then share results with their physician. The app also makes tracking lesions over time with digital monitoring easier. The app does not replace clinical dermatologist diagnoses, but physicians can use the information as supplementary material. [8]
- **Limitations:**  
  The SkinVision Service does not diagnose skin cancer or provide definitive medical diagnoses. It should not be used as a substitute for professional medical evaluation.

 NOTES: (not sure where to put this, but part of the input and output) [25]
 For the app to take a picture, the skin spot has to be focused, centered and unobstructed. The latter requirement can cause some issues with moles partly covered by hair. Such spots proved quasi-impossible to get the app to recognize and assess, even after trying to push all the hair aside as much as possible.
 SkinVision included a manual mode where pictures can be taken and assessed by a specialist in place of the app. However, sending manual photos for assessment come at an extra €14,99 for both ‘Single Smart Check’ and ‘SkinHealth Program’ options, although this service feels like it should have been included in the packages already. This aside, the manual mode only allows pictures taken on the spot to be evaluated.
 A feature that feels lacking in the current version of the app and which could be implemented in a future update is the ability to upload and assess photos of skin spots from the phone’s photo gallery. This way, pictures sent via emails and chat apps could also be assessed.
Additionally, smaller nevi with a diameter of around 1 mm proved to be quite a challenge to get in focus and pictured by the app. After numerous tries, we deemed that the app could not assess those smaller lesions.
 Moreover, for an assessment to be made, an internet connection is required. While this is understandable, an offline mode would have been welcome, or even the ability to save a case to be evaluated at a later time once the connection is established again.
- **Type & Value of Solution Output:**  
  Text-based risk indication and recommendations supporting preventive decision-making. Overall summary: User would receive a categorical risk indication per lesion (Low/medium/high) and a triage recommendation, NOT a diagnostic report. Sensitivity/specificity vary by evaluation from company/bench claims to independent studies reporting differing values. 
- **Explainability:**  
  Outputs are presented as risk categories with accompanying educational explanations; not a diagnostic report.
  Sequential, 3-step process. “The advanced technology analyses your photo using a clinically validated algorithm to provide an instant risk recommendation and advice on what to do next. Recent studies show it has an accuracy rate of 95% in detecting the most common types of skin cancer.” [23]
Step 1: Take a photo of your skin spot 
Open the app and take a photo of the skin spot that concerns you. The app will assess your photo using our clinically validated algorithm.
Step 2: Receive your risk assessment 
Within minutes, you'll receive one of three risk assessments based on the risk class including low, low (with symptoms that you have reported) or high risk.
The vast majority of our users receive immediate peace of mind through a low risk indication. If you receive a high risk indication, we will follow up with recommended next steps. This may include a visual assessment by a healthcare professional, or a note to monitor the spot closely.
We recognise that in the COVID-19 situation, there can be difficulties with visiting healthcare facilities. Not all high-risk spots are skin cancer - our data shows that in one in three high-risk cases, treatment by a healthcare professional is needed. However, it is still recommended to follow all recommendations provided by the SkinVision Service to make sure that all potential risks are considered and acted upon.
Step 3: Track your skin spots 
Securely save photos of your skin spots so you can look for changes over time. Set reminders so you can check your skin regularly. [23]

- **Foundation Models Used:**  
  Proprietary convolutional neural network architecture (details not publicly disclosed).
- **Input Data Source:**  
  User-submitted smartphone images and questionnaire responses.
  SkinVision’s model is trained primarily on user-submitted smartphone images + dermatologist assessments (with limited histology confirmation), not a standardized medical database. 
 “We have trained the SkinVision algorithm with large quantities of images which were previously assessed by our team of dermatologists. The algorithm learns which lesions are dangerous and which ones are not. We continuously train and improve our algorithm with new sets of images. From now on, all the pictures submitted through the SkinVision application go through this algorithm.” [24]
 The SkinVision app also packs some additional features like skin type assessment, risk evaluation and the UV Index based on your location. Filling out an in-app questionnaire, users can familiarize themselves with their skin type assessment. It subsequently lists the types of skin cancer that they might be at risk of according to their skin type and the precautions that should be taken. The risk evaluation determines the user’s risk of skin cancer based on factors like familial history, number of moles they have or previous issues they might have experienced with their skin. The last feature, the UV Index, gives the local UV Index on a daily basis and offers advice like sunblock use and wearing protective clothing if the index is too high. [25]
- **Output/Input Data Types:**  
  - **Input:** Digital images and structured user data  
  - **Output:** Risk classification, monitoring advice, reminders
- **Training Data:**  
  Proprietary dataset of dermatologist-reviewed smartphone images not released. 
- **Dataset Transparency:**  
  Full dataset composition not publicly disclosed. The app itself is ror all skin tones and skin types, but unsure whether the dataset is representing all ethnicities well since this is unreleased to the public. SkinVision doesn’t pull data from external clinical database. Instead, its AI model seems to be trained and validated on a proprietary dataset of skin lesion images that were:
Uploaded by app users (photos taken with their smartphone cameras)
Reviewed by dermatologists — with senior dermatologists verifying high-risk cases and junior dermatologists reviewing low-risk ones.
Biopsy/histopathology results were only available for a small subset of lesions where follow-up was possible.
For many benign lesions, the “ground truth” label was clinical dermatologist judgment from a single image, not histology.

## 5) Key Metrics

### Usefulness / Efficacy
- **Goal of Metric(s):**  
  Accurate risk stratification of skin lesions and facilitation of early clinical follow-up.
- **Reported Results:**  
  Sensitivity values reported ranging from ~73% to >95%, with specificity varying across studies.
- **Interpretation:**  
  High sensitivity supports early detection but may increase false positives.
- **Test Type:**  
  Prospective and retrospective clinical validation and peer reviewed studies.
