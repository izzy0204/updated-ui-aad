# SkinVision

## 1) Metadata
- **Model Name:**  
  [SkinVision](https://www.skinvision.com/en-us) [1]
- **Developer:**  
  Founded in 2012 by Erik de Heus headquartedin Amsterdam, the Netherlands [2]
  Company profile and developer information available at: https://www.skinvision.com/about/ [2]
- **Regulatory Approval Status**
  CE-marked medical device under EU regulation; MDR Class IIa medical device. Not FDA-approved as it is operating in area outside of United States. [7]
- **Version:**  
  SkinVision app version 6.0. Last update: 11/21/2024 [6]
- **Availability:**  
    The company has built up a customer portfolio of 1.2 million users globally and a database of 3.5 million pictures of suspicious and benign skin lesions. The SkinVision app is commercially available worldwide on iOS and Android except for the United States and Canada. SkinVision is headquartered in Amsterdam, the Netherlands. [9]
- **Summary:**  
  SkinVision is an AI mobile application designed to help people detect skin cancer early. By combining a clinically validated artificial intelligence (AI) algorithm with the expertise of leading dermatologists, the app analyzes photos of moles and skin lesions to assess the risk of skin cancer with high accuracy.[2]

## 2) Intended Use 
- **Medical Purpose**
 Analyze photos of skin lesions taken with a smartphone and classify them with a risk indication (low/medium/high) and track over a duration of time. Meant to empower the individual to self monitor skin lesions and decide when a medical followup should be considered- should not be used as a diagnostic tool ("you have melanoma").[10]
 -Provides access to skin knowledge at an accessible, scalable, and affordable price, especially when access to a dermatologist/specialized health care professional is limited. [10]
 -Diagnostic accuracy of general practitioners and family physicians in differentiating benign and malignant skin tumors is relatively low 
 -Verified, factual information pertaining to skin health can be provided on demand. [10]
 -Addresses detection of potential skin cancer due to low rates of professional screening/accessibility, and patient difficulty in recognizing lesions that could be deemed suspicious. [10]
 -Empowers patients to practice preventative self monitoring. [10]]
 -The model fits into direct-to-patient preventative care. Users can take photos of their skin at home, get instant feedback, then share results with their physician. The app also makes tracking lesions over time with digital monitoring easier. The app does not replace clinical dermatologist diagnoses, but physicians can use the information as supplementary material. [10]

 NOTES: (not sure where to put this, but part of the input and output) [29]
 For the app to take a picture, the skin spot has to be focused, centered and unobstructed. The latter requirement can cause some issues with moles partly covered by hair. Such spots proved quasi-impossible to get the app to recognize and assess, even after trying to push all the hair aside as much as possible. [29]
 SkinVision included a manual mode where pictures can be taken and assessed by a specialist in place of the app. However, sending manual photos for assessment come at an extra €14,99 for both "Single Smart Check" and "SkinHealth Program" options, although this service feels like it should have been included in the packages already. This aside, the manual mode only allows pictures taken on the spot to be evaluated. [29]
 A feature that feels lacking in the current version of the app and which could be implemented in a future update is the ability to upload and assess photos of skin spots from the phone's photo gallery. This way, pictures sent via emails and chat apps could also be assessed. [29]
 Additionally, smaller nevi with a diameter of around 1 mm proved to be quite a challenge to get in focus and pictured by the app. After numerous tries, we deemed that the app could not assess those smaller lesions. [29]
 Moreover, for an assessment to be made, an internet connection is required. While this is understandable, an offline mode would have been welcome, or even the ability to save a case to be evaluated at a later time once the connection is established again.[29]

 -Messages that could be send were either to urgently visit a doctor, visit a doctor within 4 weeks, not to worry and monitor the skin lesion, or take another picture. Use of the app was monitored during a 12-month follow-up period, including data on the CNN's assessments and teledermatologists' ratings. For this study, the risk assessments were classified as high risk when either the CNN or teledermatologist assigned high risk, or as low risk when neither the CNN nor the teledermatologist assigned high risk. - **Role in Decision Making**
 Outputs are presented as risk categories with accompanying educational explanations; not a diagnostic report.
 Sequential, 3-step process. "The advanced technology analyses your photo using a clinically validated algorithm to provide an instant risk recommendation and advice on what to do next. Recent studies show it has an accuracy rate of 95% in detecting the most common types of skin cancer." [30]
 Step 1: Take a photo of your skin spot [30]
 Open the app and take a photo of the skin spot that concerns you. The app will assess your photo using our clinically validated algorithm.
 Step 2: Receive your risk assessment [30]
 Within minutes, you'll receive one of three risk assessments based on the risk class including low, low (with symptoms that you have reported) or high risk.
 The vast majority of our users receive immediate peace of mind through a low risk indication. If you receive a high risk indication, we will follow up with recommended next steps. This may include a visual assessment by a healthcare professional, or a note to monitor the spot closely.
 We recognise that in the COVID-19 situation, there can be difficulties with visiting healthcare facilities. Not all high-risk spots are skin cancer - our data shows that in one in three high-risk cases, treatment by a healthcare professional is needed. However, it is still recommended to follow all recommendations provided by the SkinVision Service to make sure that all potential risks are considered and acted upon.
 Step 3: Track your skin spots [30]
 Securely save photos of your skin spots so you can look for changes over time. Set reminders so you can check your skin regularly. [30]

## 3) Risks and Limitations 
- **Known Performance Limitations**
-"Most lesions indicated as suspicious by the SVA (66% of the red rating) were benign naevi and seborrhoeic keratoses." [19]
 -"Sensitivity of 73% and a specificity of 83% were found for melanoma"—...sensitivty of 80% and a specificity of 78% for (pre) malignant lesions." — Certificates do not implicate good performance. [19]
 -Some of the lesions could not be analyzed by the app (45%) [19]
 -Agreement between the apps and dermatologist was limited [19]
 -This study now found that: "The algorithm scored a 95.1% (95% CI, 91.9-97.3%) sensitivity in detecting (pre)malignant conditions (93% for malignant melanoma and 97% for keratinocyte carcinomas and precursors). This level of sensitivity was achieved with a 78.3% (95% CI, 77.2-79.3%) specificity." [20]

## 4) Mechanism and Development 
- **Model Type**
 Proprietary convolutional neural network architecture (details not publicly disclosed). The proprietary dataset of dermatologist-reviewed smartphone images not released. [23]
- **Training Data**
 -Full dataset composition not publicly disclosed. The app itself is for all skin tones and skin types, but unsure whether the dataset is representing all ethnicities well since this is unreleased to the public. SkinVision doesn't pull data from external clinical database. Instead, its AI model seems to be trained and validated on a proprietary dataset of skin lesion images. [23]
 -Proprietary dataset of dermatologist-reviewed smartphone images not released. [23]
- **Model Development & Validation**
 -This SA uses a machine learning algorithm to compute a risk rating. The algorithm is trained on 131 873 images taken by 31 449 users in multiple countries between January 2016 and August 2018 and rated for risk by dermatologists. [20]
 -The company has built up a customer portfolio of 1,200,000 users globally and a database of 3.5 million pictures of suspicious and benign skin spots. [33]
- **Clinical trial**
 "The app has been clinically validated and proven to be safe and reliable. It follows international risk management standards (ISO 14971) and is built using certified quality and security systems (ISO 13485 and ISO 27001), ensuring strong product performance, data protection, and cybersecurity. An independent team of EU-appointed medical technology experts has reviewed the app and confirmed that it demonstrates consistent performance across edge cases, device types, and lighting conditions, without ever compromising user safety." [7]

 The MDR Class IIa is one of the strictest medical device standards in the world, and to receive certification under it, SkinVision had to clearly prove its medical purpose: detecting skin cancer risk through photos taken by the user's mobile phone using the SkinVision app. [39]
 Moderate Clinical Risk as an EU MDR Class IIa medical device: This means it can influence clinical decision-making, but does not directly treat or diagnose in a definitive sense [27]
 Prospective and retrospective clinical validation and peer reviewed studies is what they used to determine efficacy, linked on their website. [6]
- **Performance**
 Sensitivity values reported ranging from ~73% to >95%, with specificity varying across studies (website reports higher than other 3rd party sites) [26][6][28]
- **Interpretation:**  
  High sensitivity supports early detection but may increase false positives. [26][6][28]

In contrast, dermatologists diagnosed 1195 (99.3%) lesions as benign and identified only 9 (0.7%) as suspicious. As a result, the app classified pigmented skin lesions 27 times more frequently as suspicious than did the dermatologists, indicating a concerning rate of false positives." [28]
