# [SkinVision](https://www.skinvision.com/en-us) — Clinical Model Card

## 1) Metadata
- **Model Name:** SkinVision [1]
- **Developer:** SkinVision, founded in 2012 by Erik de Heus, headquartered in Amsterdam, the Netherlands. [2]
- **Regulatory Approval Status:** CE Marked [7]
- **Regulatory Approval Date:** Not disclosed
- **Version:** SkinVision app version 6.0. [6]
- **Last Updated:** 2024-11-21
- **Availability:** Commercially available worldwide on iOS and Android, except the United States and Canada; over 1.2 million users globally with a database of 3.5 million lesion images. [9]

## 2) Summary
- **Summary:** SkinVision is an AI-powered mobile app that lets users photograph moles and skin lesions with a smartphone and receive a low/medium/high risk indication to support preventive self-monitoring and decisions about seeking a dermatologist. It combines a clinically validated convolutional neural network algorithm with dermatologist-reviewed training images, and is not intended to provide a diagnosis. [2]

## 3) Intended Use
- **Medical Purpose:** Analyze smartphone photos of skin lesions and classify them with a low/medium/high risk indication to empower self-monitoring and inform decisions about seeking a medical follow-up; not intended as a diagnostic tool. [10]
- **Acceptable Input:** A smartphone photo of a skin lesion captured via the app's guided camera (lesion centered, in focus, area around the mole clear); users can also complete an in-app questionnaire for skin type and risk-factor assessment. [15][16]
- **Output:** A categorical risk indication (low/medium/high) per lesion plus a triage recommendation, generated by a proprietary convolutional neural network trained on dermatologist-reviewed smartphone images; not a diagnostic report. [29]
- **Role in Decision-Making:** Presented as risk categories with educational explanations to support preventive self-monitoring, not a diagnostic report; a high-risk result prompts a recommendation to seek professional evaluation but does not itself confirm skin cancer. [30]
- **Intended Users:** Individuals at risk of developing skin cancer (consumer/self-use), as well as healthcare professionals who may use the output as supplementary information; no clinical knowledge or expertise is required, only a smartphone and the app. [14][15]
- **Target Patient Population:** General consumers seeking to self-monitor skin lesions for skin cancer risk; not intended for persons under age 18. [15]

## 4) Risks and Limitations
- **Known Performance Limitations:** Independent studies report sensitivity/specificity varying by evaluation, e.g. 73% sensitivity/83% specificity for melanoma in one study and 95.1% sensitivity/78.3% specificity for (pre)malignant conditions in another; roughly 45% of lesions in one study could not be analyzed by the app; agreement between the app and dermatologists was limited in some evaluations. [19][20][21]
- **Skin Type Representation & Equity:** Full dataset composition is not publicly disclosed; the app is intended for all skin tones and types, but it is unclear whether the proprietary training dataset adequately represents all ethnicities. The company's research pipeline lists 'performance across diverse skin types and demographics' as an ongoing area of work. [23][24]
- **Required Clinician Oversight:** None required for use of the app itself; anyone with a smartphone can use it. Outputs are risk indications and triage recommendations only, not a diagnostic report, and the app explicitly recommends professional evaluation for higher-risk results. [15]

## 5) Mechanism and Development
- **Model Type:** Proprietary convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture for smartphone image-based skin lesion risk assessment. [23]
- **Training Data:** Trained and validated on a proprietary dataset of dermatologist-reviewed smartphone images; one published validation used 131,873 images from 31,449 users (Jan 2016-Aug 2018) rated for risk by dermatologists. Full dataset composition and demographic breakdown are not publicly disclosed. [20][23]
- **Model Development & Validation:** Clinically validated per ISO 14971 risk management and ISO 13485/27001 quality and security standards; reviewed by an independent team of EU-appointed medical technology experts. [Publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31494983/) [7][20]
- **Clinical Trial:** Not disclosed
- **Performance:** Reported sensitivity ranges from approximately 73% to over 95% across different independent studies, with specificity varying accordingly (78-83%); company-reported figures tend to be higher than third-party evaluations. One peer-reviewed study found 95.1% sensitivity and 78.3% specificity for detecting (pre)malignant conditions. [6][26][28]
- **Performance — Sensitivity:** 95.1% ((pre)malignant conditions, peer-reviewed study)
- **Performance — Specificity:** 78.3% ((pre)malignant conditions, peer-reviewed study)
- **Performance — Sensitivity:** 73% (melanoma, independent study)
- **Limitations in Evaluating Performance:** Sensitivity/specificity estimates vary substantially across studies and between company and independent evaluations; the proprietary training dataset's demographic composition (including skin tone representation) is not publicly disclosed, limiting evaluation of generalizability and equity. [20][23]

## 6) Governance and Monitoring
- **Plans for Ongoing Monitoring:** Algorithm is continuously updated using newly submitted user images; user data is retained up to 12 months after account deletion. [17][32]
- **Scheduled Updates:** Continuous training and improvement of the algorithm using new sets of submitted images; no fixed release schedule disclosed. [32]
