# [DermaSensor](https://www.dermasensor.com/) — Clinical Model Card

## 1) Metadata
- **Model Name:** DermaSensor [1]
- **Developer:** DermaSensor Inc. Co-founders: Dr. Maurice Ferre (Chairman) and Christopher Dewey (Director). [2]
- **Regulatory Approval Status:** FDA Cleared [8][9][10][11]
- **Regulatory Approval Date:** 2024-01-12
- **Version:** No formal software version; a handheld hardware device.
- **Last Updated:** 2026-07-25
- **Availability:** FDA Cleared and CE Marked; available for sale in Florida and Pennsylvania, with a waitlist in other U.S. states. [12][13]

## 2) Summary
- **Summary:** DermaSensor is a handheld, FDA-cleared point-of-care device that uses elastic scattering spectroscopy (ESS) and a proprietary machine learning algorithm to assess skin lesions suspicious for melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, and squamous cell carcinoma. It takes five non-invasive spectral recordings from a lesion and outputs an objective result to support non-dermatologist physicians' referral decisions. [14]

## 3) Intended Use
- **Medical Purpose:** Evaluate skin lesions suggestive of melanoma, BCC, and/or SCC in patients aged 40 and above to assist in referral decisions; used alongside clinical assessment by physicians who are not dermatologists; not a screening tool and not for sole diagnostic use. [18]
- **Acceptable Input:** Applied non-invasively to an intact skin lesion already assessed as suspicious for skin cancer; takes five short (~30 microsecond) broadband light spectral recordings (300-900nm) analyzed by an onboard microspectrometer. [5][17]
- **Output:** An objective 'Investigate Further' (positive) or 'Monitor' (negative) result; positive results include a Similarity Score from 1-10 indicating similarity to malignant lesions, generated by a proprietary neural network trained on over 2,000 histopathologically confirmed lesions. [20]
- **Role in Decision-Making:** Adjunctive referral support only, used alongside clinical assessment (including visual analysis) by physicians who are not dermatologists; does not replace clinician judgment, biopsy, or definitive diagnosis, and is not the sole diagnostic criterion. [18]
- **Intended Users:** Physicians who are not dermatologists (e.g. primary care, family medicine, internal medicine, walk-in clinics). No certification is required, but the physician must perform a clinical assessment including visual lesion analysis prior to use; Instructions for Use and supplemental training materials are provided. [5][17][20]
- **Target Patient Population:** Patients aged 40 and above with skin lesions already assessed as suspicious for skin cancer. [1]

## 4) Risks and Limitations
- **Known Performance Limitations:** False positives/negatives may lead to unnecessary referrals or missed malignant lesions. The DERM-SUCCESS study found overall device sensitivity of 95.5% (lower bound 91.7%); when used to aid PCP referral decisions, device use decreased PCPs' false negative rate from 18.0% to 8.6%. [18]
- **Skin Type Representation & Equity:** In a DERM-SUCCESS sub-analysis of 1,005 patients, 72% were Fitzpatrick I-III and 28% were Fitzpatrick IV-VI; device sensitivity (96% vs 92%) and AUROC (0.779 vs 0.764) were similar between the two groups. However, the pivotal trial population was 97.1% White and only 13% were the most pigmented skin types (Fitzpatrick V/VI); full training dataset demographics are not publicly disclosed, and the FDA has required post-market performance testing in underrepresented populations. [21][22][23]
- **Required Clinician Oversight:** Must be used by a physician (not necessarily a dermatologist) in conjunction with full clinical assessment including visual lesion analysis; not a screening tool, not the sole diagnostic criterion, and not intended for consumer or at-home use. [20]

## 5) Mechanism and Development
- **Model Type:** Predictive/risk-stratification AI/ML classifier analyzing elastic scattering spectroscopy (ESS) recordings. [20]
- **Training Data:** Proprietary neural network trained on spectral data from over 2,000 histopathologically confirmed skin lesions; full dataset composition and demographic breakdown are not publicly disclosed. [21]
- **Model Development & Validation:** Validated across three prospective clinical studies: DERM-SUCCESS Validation, DERM-ASSESS III, and DERM-SUCCESS Clinical Utility. [Publication](https://www.dermasensor.com/wp-content/uploads/Clinical-Evidence-Three-Studies.pdf) [18]
- **Clinical Trial:** DERM-SUCCESS Prospective Skin Cancer Validation Study; DERM-ASSESS III Prospective Melanoma Validation Study; DERM-SUCCESS Prospective Clinical Utility Study. [18]
- **Performance:** Device sensitivity of 95.5% (DERM-SUCCESS) was superior to study PCP investigators (83.0%); AUROC 0.803 vs 0.736 for PCPs. DERM-ASSESS III showed AUROC 0.758, comparable to dermatologists (0.747). Subgroup performance was similar across Fitzpatrick skin type groups. [18]
- **Performance — Sensitivity:** 95.5% (DERM-SUCCESS)
- **Performance — AUROC:** 0.803 (DERM-SUCCESS)
- **Performance — Specificity:** 20.7% (DERM-SUCCESS)
- **Limitations in Evaluating Performance:** Training data bias due to proprietary, non-public datasets; DERM-ASSESS III was a company-sponsored study; lack of independent, non-company-funded validation. [27][28]

## 6) Governance and Monitoring
- **Plans for Ongoing Monitoring:** Algorithm is locked per FDA requirements and not continually updated; revalidated and updated approximately every two years, requiring FDA resubmission and clearance each time. [20]
- **Scheduled Updates:** Approximately every two years, each requiring new FDA clearance; not continuously learning. [20]
