## 2) Summary
- **Summary:** DERM is an AI-based medical device that analyzes dermoscopic images of skin lesions to provide a suspected diagnosis and referral recommendation. It functions as an autonomous clinical management tool for screening, triage, and assessment of skin cancer. The model helps reduce urgent suspected cancer referrals and optimizes dermatologist capacity by issuing management instructions like 'refer', 'discharge', or 'monitor' without requiring a second human clinician review. [1]

## 3) Intended Use
- **Medical Purpose:** DERM is an artificial-intelligence (AI)-based skin lesion analysis device intended for use in the screening, triage and assessment of skin lesions suspicious for skin cancer. It helps triage patients into the right place, at the right time, and conserves dermatologist capacity. [1]
- **Acceptable Input:** Dermoscopic images of cutaneous lesions from patients aged 18 years or over, captured by approved dermoscopic hardware systems. Exclusions include lesions from patients under 18, non-cancerous skin conditions, images used for monitoring treatment or disease staging, non-dermoscopic images, images from other subjects, open/ulcerated lesions, lesions not entirely imaged, lesions obscured by hair/tattoos/scars, lesions beneath nails/in eyes/on mucosal surfaces/soles of feet/palms of hands, or previously biopsied lesions. [1]
- **Output:** The model returns a suspected diagnosis and a referral recommendation for the skin lesion, generated by artificial intelligence (AI)-based algorithms. These outputs may be used to issue management instructions such as 'refer', 'discharge', or 'monitor'. [1]
- **Role in Decision-Making:** DERM is primarily intended for healthcare organizations as an automated clinical management tool to screen, triage, or assess patients. Its outputs can directly issue management instructions (e.g., 'refer', 'discharge', 'monitor') without a second human clinician review. It can also augment other clinical data points to support clinical decision-making. [1]
- **Intended Users:** Primary care clinicians/providers (e.g., general practitioners, nurse practitioners) and secondary care clinicians/providers (e.g., dermatologists). Medical photographers and patients may capture images but are not intended users of the outputs. [1]
- **Target Patient Population:** Patients aged 18 years or over with cutaneous lesions suspicious for skin cancer, in any body location except where specific exclusions apply. [1]

## 4) Risks and Limitations
- **Known Performance Limitations:** DERM does not provide a definitive diagnosis for skin cancer. It should be used with caution on Fitzpatrick skin types V-VI and does not screen for rare malignant lesions not explicitly listed (e.g., Merkel Cell Carcinoma). Images must be dermoscopic, of sufficient quality (not too dark or blurred), and free of immersion fluid, external objects, or markers. The model only analyzes the dermoscopic image and does not incorporate other patient or lesion context captured by the client system. [1]
- **Skin Type Representation & Equity:** DERM has primarily been evaluated on patients with Fitzpatrick skin types I-IV, given the prevalence of the disease in the general population. It should be used with caution on lesions of other skin types (V-VI). [1]
- **Required Clinician Oversight:** DERM is legally authorized to independently make clinical decisions on skin cancer without oversight. However, when used to augment clinical data, the output should be viewed alongside all other clinical information before a management decision. [1]

## 5) Mechanism and Development
- **Model Type:** Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based algorithms, specifically a Deep Ensemble for the Recognition of Malignancy (DERM). [1]
- **Training Data:** Trained on a vast real-world dataset accumulated over 12 years.
- **Model Development & Validation:** Developed by Skin Analytics Limited in accordance with their ISO 13485:2016 certified Quality Management System. The system's performance is consistently comparable to specialists and has been approved by NICE for use in triage and assessment of skin lesions on the urgent suspected cancer referral pathway, while further evidence is gathered.
- **Clinical Trial:** DERM has been tested in powered prospective studies run in the UK, US, and Italy.
- **Performance:** DERM consistently performs at or above the level of specialists. It has a 99.9% Negative Predictive Value accuracy rate ruling out melanoma. Across pre-referral sites, DERM avoids Urgent Suspected Cancer Referrals (USCRs) for 60-70% of patients, with 35-50% routed towards discharge. It has helped Secondary Care organizations avoid up to 95% of NHS face-to-face USCRs.
- **Performance — Negative Predictive Value:** 99.9% (melanoma)
- **Limitations in Evaluating Performance:** DERM has been evaluated on skin lesions representative of the incidence of skin cancer in powered prospective studies in the UK, US, and Italy. Evaluation was mostly on Fitzpatrick skin types I-IV. [1]
