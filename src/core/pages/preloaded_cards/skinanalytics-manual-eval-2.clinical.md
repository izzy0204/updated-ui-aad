# [Skin Analytics](https://skin-analytics.com/) — Clinical Model Card

## 1) Metadata
- **Model Name:** Skin Analytics
- **Developer:** Skin Analytics, headquartered in the UK.
- **Regulatory Approval Status:** CE Marked
- **Regulatory Approval Date:** Not disclosed
- **Version:** SA-001165-LB; DERM-vA (version A) released July 2021.
- **Last Updated:** 2026-07-25
- **Availability:** Accessible in the UK and EU through insurance pathways (NHS or private insurance) after referral by a GP or dermatologist; not directly publicly available. Organizations register for a DERM account that integrates via API with teledermatology/EHR systems.

## 2) Summary
- **Summary:** DERM (Deep Ensemble for the Recognition of Malignancy) is Skin Analytics' Class III CE-marked AI medical device that analyzes dermoscopic images of skin lesions submitted by primary care physicians and dermatologists to give a suspected diagnosis and referral recommendation. It helps triage patients with non-cancerous lesions away from cancer pathways so dermatologist resources are used most efficiently.

## 3) Intended Use
- **Medical Purpose:** Screening, triage, and assessment of skin lesions suspicious for skin cancer, ensuring patients with non-cancerous lesions are not sent down cancer pathways and dermatologist resources are used effectively.
- **Acceptable Input:** Dermoscopic images of suspicious skin lesions captured using approved dermoscopy devices, submitted by primary care doctors or dermatologists via integrated teledermatology/EHR systems.
- **Output:** A suspected diagnosis, referral recommendation, referral recommendation guidance, and analysis explanation, generated by a deep ensemble machine-learning architecture analyzing dermoscopic images in under 20 seconds.
- **Role in Decision-Making:** May be used as an automated clinical management tool or to augment other clinical data points supporting clinical decision-making; approved to make autonomous clinical decisions without direct clinician oversight, though use is only initiated via clinician referral.
- **Intended Users:** Primary care clinicians/providers and specialty care providers (e.g. dermatologists); requires clinical training in skin lesion assessment and dermoscopy and adherence to the DERM Instructions for Use and local clinical pathways.
- **Target Patient Population:** Adults 18 years or older with skin lesions suspicious for skin cancer.

## 4) Risks and Limitations
- **Known Performance Limitations:** Performance depends on image quality, correct hardware usage, and device settings; does not provide a definitive diagnosis and does not assess rare malignant lesions outside its listed outputs; should not be used on patients under 18, for non-cancer skin conditions, for monitoring or staging, on previously biopsied lesions, or on lesions obscured by hair, tattoos, scars, beneath nails, in eyes, on mucosal surfaces, or on soles/palms.
- **Skin Type Representation & Equity:** Primarily evaluated on Fitzpatrick skin types I-IV; should be used with caution on other skin types. Real-world deployment data across 170,000 patients (all Fitzpatrick types I-VI) showed darker skin types underrepresented relative to their population share, though DERM correctly identified all melanomas, SCCs, and BCCs in Fitzpatrick V/VI patients since 2020, and subgroup analysis found no difference in performance quality for darker skin tones. However, darker skin types remain underrepresented in the training dataset (only 2.2% Fitzpatrick IV-VI in the DERM-003 study), limiting generalizability, and DERM cannot currently assess palmoplantar/subungual lesions, which disproportionately affect darker-skinned patients.
- **Required Clinician Oversight:** Approved to make autonomous clinical decisions without direct clinician oversight; however, use of the platform can only be initiated via a clinician through medical referral, not directly or publicly by patients.

## 5) Mechanism and Development
- **Model Type:** Deep ensemble machine-learning architecture for dermoscopic image analysis.
- **Training Data:** Training dataset composition not publicly specified.
- **Model Development & Validation:** Peer-reviewed validation studies have been published on DERM's performance. [Publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37869160/)
- **Clinical Trial:** NCT04116983 — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04116983) — Additional related trials: NCT04123678, NCT05126173.
- **Performance:** DERM achieved AUROC of 91.8-95.8% for melanoma detection across camera devices, with 99.8% negative predictive value (vs. 98.9% for dermatologists); AUROC 0.85-0.89 for SCC and 0.87-0.89 for BCC. In real-world deployment across 170,000+ patients, DERM demonstrated 97% pathway sensitivity for malignant melanoma, exceeding the 95% target, with no adverse safety events or missed high-risk cancers reported. Subgroup performance across Fitzpatrick skin types I-VI showed no meaningful difference in detection quality.
- **Performance — AUROC (melanoma):** 91.8-95.8%
- **Performance — NPV (melanoma):** 99.8%
- **Performance — Pathway sensitivity:** 97% (real-world deployment, 170,000+ patients)
- **Limitations in Evaluating Performance:** Not disclosed
