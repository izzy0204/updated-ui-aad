## 2) Summary
- **Summary:** DermaSensor is an AI-powered, software-aided adjunctive diagnostic device that uses optical spectroscopy, specifically Elastic Scattering Spectroscopy (ESS), and an artificial intelligence/machine learning (AI/ML) algorithm to non-invasively analyze intact skin lesions. It is indicated for use by physicians who are not dermatologists to evaluate skin lesions suggestive of melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, and/or squamous cell carcinoma in patients aged 40 and above, assisting in the decision regarding referral to a dermatologist. The device provides results as "Monitor" (negative) or "Investigate Further" (positive) with an additional Similarity Score of 1-10 for positive outputs. It is intended to be used in conjunction with clinical assessment and is not a standalone diagnostic tool or a screening tool.

## 5) Mechanism and Development
- **Model Type:** The DermaSensor device utilizes an artificial intelligence/machine learning (AI/ML) based software algorithm, specifically a proprietary ML-derived classifier algorithm, to analyze Elastic Scattering Spectroscopy (ESS) spectral recordings. The DermaSensor device is a software-aided adjunctive diagnostic device that utilizes optical spectroscopy and an artificial intelligence/machine learning (AI/ML) based software algorithm to analyze intact skin lesions non-invasively. It is a combination of a handheld unit and a base unit. The handheld unit contains a xenon arc lamp and a fiber-optic probe tip that transmits broadband white light to a lesion surface. It collects backscattered light samples via an adjacent detection optical fiber, conveying them to a microspectrometer to yield Elastic Scattering Spectroscopy (ESS) spectral recordings. The handheld unit features a touch-screen interface for operation. The base unit recharges the handheld unit wirelessly and contains calibration material. An internal microprocessor and proprietary ML-derived classifier algorithm analyze the optical recordings to assess for the potential presence of melanoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or basal cell carcinoma. The device operates with Software Version 3.0.2.
- **Training Data:** The training dataset contained 950 lesions, contributing approximately 4,200 Elastic Scattering Spectroscopy (ESS) spectra. The algorithm was trained in the spectral range of 360 to 810 nm, using 47 selected wavelengths to reduce dimensionality. Patient demographics for the training dataset were: Age (20-39: 7.6%, 40-59: 8.2%, 60-79: 56.6%, 80+: 4.2%, Unknown: 23.4%); Fitzpatrick Phototype (I: 6.3%, II: 31.1%, III: 22.9%, IV: 4.3%, V: 0.7%, VI: 0.3%, Unknown: 34.4%); Ethnicity/Race (White: 73.5%, Black or African American: 0.5%, Asian: 0.8%, Multiracial: 0.2%, Native Hawaiian or Other: 1.6%, Unknown: 23.4%); Gender (Male: 39.7%, Female: 24.8%, Unknown: 35.4%). Anatomical locations included sun-exposed areas like head/neck (5.9%), chest/upper back (3.3%), forearms/hands (3.5%), lower legs (1.4%), and unexposed areas like buttocks/hips (0.1%), upper legs (1.1%), lower trunk (0.7%). The diagnoses included melanoma and severely dysplastic nevi (8.2%), SCC (9.0%), BCC (12.8%), mixed malignant (0.1%), and various benign mimics such as SK (brown) (6.9%), Benign Nevi (27.5%), AK (7.5%), SK (nonbrown) (3.3%), and other benign/malignant lesions. - **Model Development & Validation:** The algorithm was developed and tested using a dataset that included 950 lesions (contributing ~4,200 spectra) for training and 361 independent, randomly chosen lesions for testing, totaling 1,311 skin lesions. The training and tuning data predominantly included older patients (age 60-79), Fitzpatrick phototypes I-III, and individuals who self-identified as White, reflecting the higher incidence of skin cancer in these populations. Lesions on palms, soles, and under nails were excluded. The anatomical locations were primarily head and neck, followed by chest/upper back and forearms/hands. The diagnoses included BCC, SCC, melanoma (and severely atypical nevi), and various benign mimics. For postmarket surveillance, FDA requires collection and reporting of data from clinical validation performance testing in patients from demographic groups representative of the U.S. population, specifically to include populations with limited representation in premarket studies (e.g., Fitzpatrick skin phototypes IV, V, and VI) or where sensitivity was below 90%. This postmarket study requires a statistically justified sample size to confirm non-inferior performance, with overrepresentation of Fitzpatrick skin phototypes with lower prevalence of skin cancer, and recording of age, sex, socioeconomic status, race, and risk factors for skin cancer development and progression. - **Clinical Trial:** Clinical development for the DermaSensor device included a blinded, multicenter clinical study through an initial ML retrospective algorithm, followed by a subsequent blinded, multicenter clinical study. These studies investigated the performance of an algorithm using Elastic Scattering Spectroscopy (ESS) and Machine Learning (ML) for common types of skin cancers. The initial study dataset was later used entirely for algorithm training, comprising 950 lesions for training and 361 lesions for testing, totaling 1,311 skin lesions. The training and testing datasets were independent and chosen randomly. As part of the FDA De Novo classification, the developer is required to conduct postmarket clinical validation performance testing in patients from demographic groups representative of the U.S. population, including populations with limited representation of melanomas in premarket studies (e.g., Fitzpatrick skin phototypes IV, V, and VI) or where sensitivity was lower than 90%. This postmarket study must include a statistically justified sample size to confirm device performance, enroll a representative range of subjects with overrepresentation of Fitzpatrick skin phototypes IV, V, and VI, and record age, sex, socioeconomic status, race, and skin cancer risk factors to assess their impact on the device’s effectiveness. The study protocol was required to be submitted within 30 days of the January 12, 2024 order, with specific enrollment milestones: first subject enrolled within 6 months, 20% within 12 months, 50% within 24 months, and 100% within 36 months from protocol approval. Periodic progress reports are also required.

These findings provide reasonable assurance of the device's safety and effectiveness when used as intended. Standalone device performance testing must demonstrate at least 90% sensitivity of the device output for lesions with high metastatic potential. When pooling lesions across tuning datasets, high sensitivity (at or above 90%) was observed for each of the three individual cancers.
