## 2) Summary
- **Summary:** DermGPT is an advanced web-based AI assistant designed to help healthcare providers, particularly dermatologists, with clinical tasks to increase productivity. It utilizes a learned language model based on over 3000 peer-reviewed dermatology articles and texts. The tool offers AI-powered skin analysis via image classification (using models like MobileNet, ResNet, ViT), providing high-accuracy detection of various skin conditions (rashes, moles, lesions). It includes visual explanations (Grad-CAM, saliency maps, confidence scores) for interpretable AI. Leveraging GPT-4, it provides natural language insights, explanations of detected conditions, treatment recommendations, care suggestions, and follow-up questions for dermatologist consultation. For evidence-based support, it offers RAG-powered retrieval from PubMed abstracts, including supporting scientific literature, citations, and source tracking. Its search output includes citations, and it aims to reduce hallucinations by sourcing answers from a specialized research database.

## 5) Mechanism and Development
- **Model Type:** DermGPT is a hybrid AI model that integrates a learned large language model (LLM) with computer vision capabilities. It functions as an AI-driven chatbot and web-based assistant. Specifically, it uses a GPT base model (GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 are mentioned for different components) for natural language processing and pretrained vision models (MobileNet, ResNet, ViT, and YOLOv8) for image classification and skin analysis. DermGPT is an AI-driven chatbot and advanced web-based AI assistant specifically designed for dermatology, aiming to help healthcare providers with clinical tasks to increase productivity and monitor skin health. It analyzes skin conditions using computer vision and natural language processing. The system provides detailed analysis, confidence scores, visual explanations (Grad-CAM), and treatment recommendations. It integrates a learned language model (GPT-3.5 or GPT-4, depending on the component) with computer vision models (YOLOv8, MobileNet, ResNet, ViT) and a research database (PubMed abstracts) to source answers and provide citations, thereby aiming to reduce 'hallucinations'. - **Model Development & Validation:** A comparative study evaluated DermGPT against ChatGPT using a double-blind method. Two dermatology residents authored a list of questions, which were then posed to each LLM. The models' answers for each question were paired and randomized as A or B. A survey was distributed to dermatologists at the University of California, Irvine, and the University of California, Davis. The raters, comprising 13 attending physicians and 6 residents/fellows (total n=19), were blinded to which model produced which response and were asked to choose preferred answers based on quality, suitability for patient care, or accuracy. Three questions where DermGPT issued disclaimers instead of direct answers were excluded from the evaluation. - **Clinical Trial:** A double-blind study compared DermGPT's responses to ChatGPT's for dermatology-related queries. The study involved a voluntary, anonymous survey distributed to dermatologists (faculty and residents) at the University of California, Irvine, and the University of California, Davis. A total of 19 responses were received, comprising 13 attending physicians and 6 residents or fellows, corresponding to an overall response rate of approximately 20%. Participants were blinded to which model produced which response and asked to choose preferred answers based on quality, suitability for patient care, or accuracy. Overall, DermGPT's answers (48.1%) were preferred over ChatGPT's (28.4%) with a statistically significant χ2 test (P=.04). However, the study was constrained by a small rater sample (n=19) and multiple ratings per rater and question, so P values are considered exploratory rather than confirmatory, limiting generalizability.
- **Performance:** In a comparative study, ChatGPT's references were preferred overall (46%) over DermGPT's (23.5%), though the χ2 test for references was not significant (P=.50). In the attending group, ChatGPT references were preferred (48.2%) over DermGPT (23.1%), and similarly in the resident group (ChatGPT: 41.1% vs. DermGPT: 24.4%). For three questions in the study, DermGPT issued disclaimers instead of direct answers, recommending consultation with a dermatologist or guidelines, leading to their exclusion from evaluation. DermGPT showed strong potential for improving answer clarity and conciseness in dermatology-related queries, making its responses accessible for quick clinical reference. However, ChatGPT was favored for providing more robust source citations, which enhanced user trust and perceived academic rigor. ChatGPT consistently cited reputable references, such as the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology and the Journal of the American Medical Association, which contributed to its credibility. These findings suggest the importance of balancing clarity with strong citation practices in AI-assisted medical tools. The model's occasional disclaimers, recommending professional consultation, indicate a built-in safety mechanism, although the developer also explicitly warns users to proofread all content due to the potential for inaccuracies. In a comparative study against ChatGPT, DermGPT's answers were preferred overall by 48.1% of respondents compared to ChatGPT's 28.4%, with the χ2 test showing significance (P=.04). Specifically, in the attending physician group (n=195 responses), DermGPT's answers were preferred 47.7% of the time (93 responses) compared to ChatGPT's 28.7% (56 responses). In the resident group (n=90 responses), DermGPT was preferred 48.9% of the time (44 responses) versus ChatGPT's 27.8% (25 responses). In Dr. Kamangar's practice, DermGPT has been able to save at least 30 to 60 minutes at the end of the day typically spent updating electronic health records.
