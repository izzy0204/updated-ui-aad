## 2) Uses & Directions
- **Intended Use & Workflow:** The DermaSensor device is indicated for use to evaluate skin lesions suggestive of melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, and/or squamous cell carcinoma in patients aged 40 and above. [4,5] Its primary purpose is to assist physicians who are not dermatologists in the decision regarding referral of the patient to a dermatologist. [4,5] It should be used in conjunction with the totality of clinically relevant information from the clinical assessment, including visual analysis of the lesion. [4,5] The device should be used on lesions already assessed as suspicious for skin cancer and not as a screening tool. [4,5] It is not intended for use as the sole diagnostic criterion, to confirm a clinical diagnosis of skin cancer, or as a stand-alone diagnostic device. [4,5] It is also not intended to replace biopsy or clinical decision-making. [4,5] The device utilizes optical spectroscopy and an AI/ML-based software algorithm to analyze an intact skin lesion non-invasively, providing results as "Monitor" or "Investigate Further," with a Similarity Score for positive results. [4,5]
- **Primary Intended Users:** Physicians who are not dermatologists. [4]
- **Necessary knowledge/expertise:** Users must be physicians, though not necessarily dermatologists, and are expected to perform a clinical assessment, including visual analysis of the lesion, prior to using the device. [4,5] The device is a prescription device, restricting its sale, distribution, and use in accordance with 21 CFR 801.109. [4,5]
- **How to Use:** The DermaSensor device is a combination of a handheld unit and a base unit. [5] The handheld unit, featuring a touch-screen interface, is operated with step-by-step guidance. [5] A small fiber-optic tip, containing a xenon arc lamp and a fiber-optic probe, transmits broadband white light to the lesion surface and collects backscattered light. [5] This process yields Elastic Scattering Spectroscopy (ESS) spectral recordings. [5] An internal microprocessor and proprietary ML-derived classifier algorithm analyze these recordings to assess the potential presence of melanoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or basal cell carcinoma. [5] Results are provided as "Monitor" (negative) or "Investigate Further" (positive). [5] For positive outputs, a Similarity Score of 1-10 is displayed, indicating greater similarity to malignant lesions. [5] The handheld unit's battery recharges wirelessly in the base unit, which also contains calibration material. [5] The device tip contacts intact skin for less than 3 minutes, and the user remains gloved during operation. [5]
- **Targeted patient population:** Patients aged 40 and above with skin lesions suggestive of melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, and/or squamous cell carcinoma. [4,5] The device is intended for use on primary lesions only. [4,5] The training and tuning data for the algorithm included a majority of older patients (age 60-79), Fitzpatrick phototype I-III, and individuals who self-identified as White, reflecting the higher incidence of skin cancer in these populations. [4,5]

## 5) Key Metrics
### Usefulness / Efficacy
- **Goal of Metric(s):** The goal of efficacy metrics is to demonstrate the superior accuracy of device-aided users' diagnostic characterization of indicated lesions compared to un-aided users, and to demonstrate the accuracy of the device output relative to ground truth. [4] Specifically, the device output should demonstrate at least 90% sensitivity for lesions with high metastatic potential, or a justified alternative clinical consideration for lower sensitivity. [4] Clinical justification must also be provided for the reported specificity. [4] The overall goal is to assist physicians who are not dermatologists in the decision regarding referral of patients aged 40 and above with skin lesions suggestive of melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, and/or squamous cell carcinoma. [4]
- **Result:** In algorithm tuning, the sensitivity estimates were 96.5% (221/229). [4,5] Specificity for physician-biopsied lesions was 20.6% (28/136), and for physician-selected un-biopsied lesions was 33.0% (128/388). [4,5] When pooling lesions across tuning datasets, high sensitivity (at or above 90%) was observed for each of the three individual cancers (melanoma, SCC, BCC). [4,5] The associated specificity of 20.6% was clinically accepted due to being paired with high sensitivity and the greater benefit of identifying skin cancer outweighing the risk of excess referrals. [4,5] The FDA determined that, for the stated indications for use, the DermaSensor can be classified in Class II with special controls that provide reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness, including requirements for clinical performance testing to demonstrate superior accuracy of device-aided users compared to unaided users. [4,5]
- **Interpretation:** The high sensitivity (≥90%) observed during algorithm tuning for melanoma, SCC, and BCC indicates the device's strong ability to correctly identify malignant lesions. [4,5] While the specificity (20.6% for biopsied, 33.0% for un-biopsied) is lower, it was deemed clinically acceptable because the benefit of identifying skin cancer outweighs the risk of unnecessary referrals, especially when used as an adjunctive tool by non-dermatologist physicians. [4,5] The FDA's classification in Class II with special controls implies that, with proper adherence to these controls, the device can provide reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness for its intended use as an adjunctive diagnostic aid. [4,5]
- **Test Type:** Usefulness and efficacy were evaluated through several types of testing: blinded, multicenter clinical studies for algorithm development and performance investigation; non-clinical performance testing (bench testing) to ensure the device performs as expected under anticipated conditions of use; and standalone device performance testing to demonstrate the accuracy of the device output relative to ground truth. [4,5] The FDA also mandated postmarket clinical validation performance testing in patients from demographic groups representative of the U.S. [4,5] population to confirm that performance in post-market use is not inferior to pre-market study results. [4,5]
- **Testing Data Description:** Premarket clinical performance validation testing data included a training dataset of 950 lesions (~4,200 spectra) and a testing dataset of 361 lesions, totaling 1,311 skin lesions from a blinded, multicenter clinical study.
